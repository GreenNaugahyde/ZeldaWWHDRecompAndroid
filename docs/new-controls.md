Version 0.9.6 brings new on-screen controls, a setup for game controllers, an item ring, touch
controls for the minigames and new ways to conduct the Wind Waker's songs. This page explains every
option and how they work together. Everything here can be changed at any time, and almost
everything can be switched back to the game's original behaviour.

## The first-start questions

The first time the game starts with 0.9.6 (new installation or update), three questions come before the
title screen, once:

1. **New touch controls**: *Use the new controls* sets up everything below ready to play; *Keep Classic*
   changes nothing. The new controls are:
   - the **Flexible** style with invisible sticks (the left thumb moves Link wherever it lands),
     the **swipe camera** on the right (speed +100) with the direct camera, and the original
     walking direction;
   - **context buttons**, **items by tap** with only the aiming buttons while aiming, **ZL** moving on
     to the next target;
   - the **item ring**, **minigames by touch**, **hearts only in combat**, **rupees only when they
     change**, the **large Picto Box view**, **replayed conducted songs**, play buttons in the song
     list, motion controls and rumble;
   - the **arc layout**: the bottom right corner is free for the swipe camera; around it on an inner
     arc, from the top: Jump, A1, A and B; on an outer arc: Crouch (above Jump), A2, the moon jump and
     A3. R3 sits between + and B, ZL in the middle of the left edge, the d-pad at the top left.
2. **Jump anywhere**: Yes switches it on and asks about the **double jump**.
3. **Sing the songs**: *Try singing* switches singing on (Android asks for the microphone); *Note pads*
   keeps conducting with the two pads.

Every answer can be changed later in the options, and the layout in the layout editor.

## Where the options are now

![The options in the game's style](screenshots/options-controls.jpg)

The options menu now looks like the game's own GamePad menus (its paper, tabs, buttons and font,
taken from your game files) and speaks the game's language: English, German, French, Italian,
Spanish or Japanese, whichever you play the game in.

**The ≡ menu button is gone by default.** The options are now a page of the game's **+ menu**:

1. Press **+** (bottom centre on the touch controls, Start on a controller). The game's GamePad
   screen with your items opens.
2. Left of the items page there is a page arrow, drawn like the game's own. Tap it, or swipe the
   pages to the right, and the options slide in from the left.
3. Its right arrow, B or + take you back to the items and the game.

With a controller, the game's bracket cursor moves over the options page: L / R switch tabs, the
d-pad moves and changes values, pressing towards a big page arrow turns the page.

Other ways to open the options over the game, as before:

- **Android's Back** gesture or button,
- on a controller **hold Select** (View, Share, −) for a moment, or press **Home / Guide**.

If you prefer the old menu button: Controls › Touch › **Menu button in the game**: On brings back
the ≡ button at the top left, and the options open over the game instead of in the + menu.

The menu's tabs:

| Tab | What is there |
|---|---|
| **Saves** | save states, auto save, last session, what happens at start, import / export |
| **Game** | language, quick doors, fast scene changes, fast forward, Songs |
| **Graphics** | preset, best-settings test, resolution, frame rate, frame generation, and pages for resolution, picture quality, screens, performance overlay, advanced |
| **Mods** | jump anywhere, moon jump, climbing, faster moving, cheats, mod packs |
| **Controls** | touch style, layout editor, motion controls, rumble, and the pages Touch, Item ring, Camera, Minigames & hints, Screen (HUD), Controller |

## The two touch styles: Classic and Flexible

![Flexible touch controls with context buttons](screenshots/touch-controls.jpg)

Controls › **Touch controls** chooses the style.

**Classic** (the default) is a fixed on-screen GamePad: both sticks, the d-pad, A / B / X / Y, L / R,
ZL / ZR, + and the stick clicks, always in the same places. The game's own button display (the
A / B / X / Y / R icons at the top right) stays as in the game. Choose this if you know the
original controls and want them as they are.

**Flexible** puts the sticks where your thumbs land:

- a thumb on the **left half** of the screen is the movement stick, centred where it touched;
- a thumb on the **right half** is the camera: a stick there as well, or a swipe camera that turns
  with your finger (Controls › Camera › **Camera (right half)**: Stick or Swipe);
- **Show sticks** (Controls › Touch): Off hides the stick graphics, so you control from a blank
  screen.

Flexible also unlocks the options that make the screen change with the situation: **context
buttons**, **items by tap** and the **large Picto Box view** (below).

In both styles:

- **Left stick follows the thumb** (Controls › Touch, on by default): a thumb that slides past the
  left stick's edge pulls the stick along, so you don't lose it in a long run. Off: the stick keeps
  its place.
- **On-screen controls size** (Controls › Touch): 75 % to 130 %.
- The controls hide while a game controller is in use and come back on the next touch.
- **Link in the button icons** (Controls › Touch, on by default): the Jump, Crouch, Stand up and Roll
  buttons show Link himself in that pose, drawn from the game's own model on your device (the roll
  plays). Sidle, Lift, Throw, Put down, Open (a door or a chest), Grab / Push and Pick up show him
  doing it, animated, with the pot, chest, door or block. Off: plain pictograms.

## Context buttons

Controls › Touch › **Context buttons** (Flexible only, off by default).

With context buttons the game's own button display is hidden, and A, B, X, Y, R and ZR appear only
when they do something, each showing **what** it does in the game's own icons and words: Talk,
Open, Lift, Throw, Next, Put away, Drop, Swim faster, the item on X / Y / R, and so on. Things that
can't be used right now (dimmed in the game) don't show at all.

How they behave in the main situations:

- **Walking**: the sword on B, the items on X / Y / R, A only when there is something to do, the
  d-pad's up (Wind Waker) while Link has it. ZL targets; tapped once it latches (stays on) until you
  tap it again.
- **Talking, reading, cutscenes**: A becomes **Next**. In cutscenes without text and during doors
  and fades all buttons hide; with Game › Fast forward cutscenes on, ZR stays to fast forward.
- **Carrying** something: A throws, B drops.
- **Swimming**: only ZL and + (and A when the game offers an action); the sword, items, first person
  and jumps can't be used in water and hide.
- **Picto Box and telescope**: only their own buttons (shutter, album, selfie, back); the item
  buttons, d-pad and jumps hide.
- **Item in hand** (Link holds the bow, the Wind Waker, the Deku Leaf ...): the put-away button shows
  that item with a down arrow.
- **Minigames**: only B and + stay (the minigame is played on the picture, see below).

Related options on Controls › Touch:

- **ZL when the target is defeated**: with a tapped ZL, **Let go** (default) releases the lock once
  the enemy is defeated; **Next target** keeps ZL on and locks the next enemy in reach.
- **Link in the button icons**, see above.

## Items by tap

Controls › Touch › **Item buttons** (Flexible with context buttons): **Tap** (default) or **Hold**.

The bow, boomerang, hookshot and grappling hook normally need their button held while you aim. With
**Tap**:

1. Tap the item's button: Link takes it out and aims (it stays out, no need to hold).
2. The button turns into **Shoot**: each tap fires.
3. **ZL** switches between first and third person while aiming; ZR picks the arrow type where the
   game offers it.
4. **B** puts the item away.

**Only aiming buttons while aiming** (on by default): while an item is aimed only Shoot, B, ZL and
ZR show; the other items, A, +, first person, the d-pad and the jump buttons hide, so a stray tap
can't make you lose a drawn arrow.

Also with Tap:

- The **sword** goes back on its own 5 seconds after the last swing (walking counts as activity), so
  you don't run around with the sword drawn.
- The **Deku Leaf**: B keeps the sword; the leaf goes away by itself once Link stands still. While
  gliding a tap on its button closes the leaf and drops, another tap opens it again; **holding** the
  button waves the leaf (a gust).

**Hold** works exactly as in the game.

## The item ring

![The item ring](screenshots/item-ring.jpg)

Controls › **Item ring** (on by default), for touch and controllers.

**Hold X, Y or R** (on screen or on a controller) and a ring of all your items opens around it. Slide
to an item (or tilt the left stick towards it) and let go: it goes onto that button. A short tap still
uses the item as before.

- **While the ring is open** the game keeps running, plays in **slow motion** (default) or pauses.
- **Bags** (spoils, bait, delivery) open as rings of their contents.
- The **Wind Waker** picked from the ring makes Link conduct right away; it doesn't take the button.
- On the **boat** the ring also has the cannon and the salvage arm.
- The ring has an **empty** entry (a struck-through circle) while the button has an item: pick it to
  leave the button empty.

With the item ring on in the Flexible style and no controller connected, the d-pad hides in play: the
ring has what it did (Wind Waker, cannon, salvage arm).

## Item buttons: names, L1 and d-pad down, emptying

**Item button names** (Controls › Item ring): the game calls its item buttons X, Y and R. The app can
rename them on the game's HUD and on the GamePad's item slots:

- **Auto** (default): A1, A2, A3 while you play by touch; with a controller, the name of the
  controller button that presses it;
- **A1, A2, A3** always;
- **X, Y, R** as in the game.

**Emptying a button**: on the item page (+), with touch, hold a finger on an item button's slot for
0.6 s (a short vibration confirms); with a controller, press an item button while the cursor is on
the item already on it; or pick the empty entry of the item ring.

**L1 and d-pad down as item buttons** (Controls › Controller › Controller buttons, for controllers):
the game doesn't use L1 or d-pad down while playing, so either can take over item button X, Y or R
(with its item, its slot on the item page and the item ring). Useful when Jump anywhere took a face
button. Then:

- L1 no longer presses the Wii U's L: no page turns on the item page with L1, and L1 no longer holds
  the singing mic button.
- d-pad down is the item button only while playing; in menus it is d-pad down. On the item page,
  hold ZL and press d-pad down to put the item under the cursor onto it.

## Jump anywhere and the moon jump on their own buttons

Mods › **Jump anywhere** and Mods › **Moon jump** used to share L. Now each has its own input:

- **Touch**: each has a button of its own while the mod is on (Jump beside ZL / at L's place, the moon
  jump beside it), hidden in cutscenes, the Picto Box and the telescope. Jump anywhere doesn't jump
  out of water, so its button hides while swimming.
- **Controller**: Mods › Jump anywhere › **Controller button** (and the same for the moon jump)
  picks the button, L1 by default. Both can be on at once on different buttons; on the same button
  the moon jump wins.

If you choose a button the game uses while playing, the app asks what to do with that button's own
function:

- **Move**: it goes to another free button (L1 / d-pad down, which the game doesn't use in play);
- **Move to L1 / Move to D-pad down** for an item button: the item button goes there with its item;
- **Take off**: no controller button presses it any more (for an item button: that item can't be
  used from the controller, the other two can; d-pad up / left / right can be taken off while the
  item ring is on, as the ring has the Wind Waker, cannon and salvage arm).

The controller setup below offers the same as ready-made layouts.

## Editing the layout

![The layout editor](screenshots/layout-editor.jpg)

Controls › **Edit on-screen layout** shows the controls over the game with a toolbar:

- **Drag** a control to move it, **pinch** or Size − / + to resize, **tap** to select.
- **Grid** on: controls lock onto a grid that is symmetric around the screen's centre, so left and
  right places mirror each other.
- **Hide / Show** the selected control (the menu button stays).
- **Shape**: standard, round, oval, square, rectangle, hexagon, diamond, per button.
- **Opacity**: a slider (5 % to 100 %) for the selected control, or for all when none is selected
  (tap an empty spot).
- **Style**: filled or outline.
- **Presets**: save the layout under a name, load, delete, export one or all to a file and import
  them (on another device, for example). A layout belongs to its style (Classic or Flexible); loading
  one of the other style offers to switch.
- **Reset** brings back the style's default layout; **Done** keeps your changes.

Each style keeps its own layout.

## Camera options

Controls › **Camera**:

- **Invert camera up/down**: Off, only in first person and aiming, or always.
- **Direct right-stick camera** (from the original project): no easing, the camera follows the stick
  at once; **Direct camera speed** 0.5× to 2×. On the boat it now turns as fast as on foot.
- **First person on the mouse wheel**: a mouse wheel switches to first person (R3 is the game's own
  first person look either way).
- **Walking direction**: what happens to Link's direction when the camera turns while you walk.
  - *Follows the camera* (original): Link turns with the camera.
  - *Kept when you turn the camera*: turning the camera yourself doesn't turn Link; moving the stick
    (or letting go) walks relative to the camera again.
  - *Kept while the stick is held*: every camera turn (also the game's own) keeps Link's direction
    as long as you hold the stick. **Stick re-syncs after** (1° to 20°, default 1°): how far the stick
    moves before Link walks relative to the camera again.
  - **Camera swings behind Link** (with either "Kept" mode): while he runs and you leave the right
    stick alone, the camera swings round behind him; **Swing starts after** 0.5 to 3 s.
- **Camera (right half)** (Flexible): Stick or Swipe. With Swipe: **Swipe speed**, and **Swipe speed
  on the boat** (1× to 4×) as the boat's camera turns slowly.

**Field of view** (Graphics › Screens): zoom the view out (Original up to 2×) to see more around
Link. A two-finger pinch on the picture changes it too.

touchable where the Wii U had buttons:

| Where | By touch |

| **Sploosh Kaboom** | touch a square to aim, slide to another, lift the finger to fire |
| **Picture puzzles** (Cabana) | tap a piece in line with the gap, or drag it toward the gap: whole rows move |
| **Mail sorting** | tap the slot with the letter's sign |
| **Spectacle Island cannon** | drag to aim, tap to fire |
| **Auction** | tap anywhere to raise your bid |
| **Number entry** | swipe up / down to change a digit, left / right to pick another, tap to confirm |
| **Answers** in a message | touch the line you want to say |
| **Shops** | touch an item to look at it, touch it again to buy |
| **Nintendo Gallery figures** | drag to turn a figure, pinch to look closer |

| **Item pages** (+) | swipe sideways to turn the pages |

again shows every hint once more. A hint can also be answered with a controller or a keyboard:
up / down moves the cursor, A takes the marked line, B closes it.

## Conducting the songs

The Wind Waker's songs are played by conducting: Link swings the baton in one of three times
(3/4, 4/4, 6/4), and each beat takes one of five directions (centre, up, right, down, left). 0.9.6
offers several ways to do it, from the original to no conducting at all.

### With a controller (original)

The left stick picks the time (centre 3/4, left 4/4, right 6/4), the right stick (C stick) swings
the baton on each beat.

### By touch with the note pads (default)

![Note pads for conducting](screenshots/note-pads.jpg)

Controls › Minigames & hints › **Note pads for conducting** (on). While Link conducts, two pads show:

- **Right pad**: the game's five note signs as a d-pad (centre, up, right, down, left). Touch or
  slide to a sign to swing the baton that way on the beat; let go for the centre.
- **Left pad**: the three times (4/4, 3/4, 6/4); tap one to choose it.

B ends conducting (its icon shows the baton pointing down). Off: a finger on each half of the
picture works as a stick (left half: the time, right half: the baton).

### Play buttons, no conducting needed

![Play buttons in the song list](screenshots/song-list.jpg)

Game › Songs:

- **Play buttons in the song list** (on): while Link holds the baton, + shows the songs he has
  learned, each with a round play button; a tap plays that song at once.
- **Play button in song scenes** (on): where the story waits for one particular song (learning it at
  a stone tablet or from a teacher, playing it for a sage), a play button with the song's name sits in
  the message box and plays it, learned or not.

### Singing (experimental)

![Singing: the practice timeline](screenshots/singing.jpg)

Game › Songs › **Sing the songs** (off by default; Android asks for the microphone permission). While
Link holds the Wind Waker, sing, hum or whistle a song he has learned and he plays it. The sound is
only analysed on the device, never recorded or sent anywhere (the app has no internet access).

With **Mic button** (on by default):

1. Take out the Wind Waker. A **mic button** shows at the bottom left, a **time switch** (3/4, 4/4,
   6/4) above it.
2. Pick the song's time with the switch.
3. Hold the mic button (or **L** on a controller). A **count-in** of two bars plays with the game's
   own metronome: during the first bar, hum your **middle note**; the song's notes are placed around
   it.
4. Sing the song, one note per beat. A timeline under the game's time bar shows the pitch you sing
   and each note the game takes.
5. Once a song's worth of notes is sung, Link plays it.

While conducting by voice the view zooms out a little, so Link stays visible below the timeline.

Mic button off: the microphone listens by itself while Link holds the Wind Waker, and a short pause
after the last note ends a song.

**Practice**: the first time, a hint offers to try it: you sing and see every note you hit, but Link
plays nothing. Once a learned song is recognised, the app asks whether to play it for real.

### Replay conducted songs

Game › Songs › **Replay conducted songs** (off): as on the GameCube, after you conduct a song Link
plays it once more before it takes effect. The Wii U version goes straight to the effect.

## Game controllers

### The setup

When a controller is used for the first time, the app offers **Set up controller** (Set up / Later /
Don't ask). Run it any time from Controls › Controller › **Set up controller**. Its steps:

1. **What it is about**: the game knows only the Wii U GamePad; each of its buttons is pressed by one
   of yours, and each controller keeps its own assignment.
2. **Face buttons**:
   - *By position*: as on the Wii U GamePad. The bottom button is the sword (the Wii U's B), the
     right one the action button (A); your controller's letters then differ from the game's.
   - *By letters*: your controller's A is the game's A (action), B the sword, X and Y the item
     buttons A2 and A1.
3. **Layout**:
   - *Original*: every button as on the Wii U; Jump anywhere and the moon jump (when on) on L1 / LB,
     which the game doesn't use while playing.
   - *Jump on an item button, the item off*: Jump anywhere on one of the item buttons; that item can't
     be used from the controller, the other two can. Switches Jump anywhere on.
   - *Jump on any button, its use to L*: Jump anywhere on a button of your choice (a face button, for
     example); what it did moves to L1 / LB, so nothing is lost. For an item button, L1 becomes that
     item button. Switches Jump anywhere on.
4. **Button for jumping** (for the last two): press the button that should jump.
5. **Try it**: press any button and it lights up with what it does in the game. Hold Start /
   Options to finish.

### Single buttons

Controls › Controller › **Controller buttons**: choose a Wii U button, then press the controller
button for it (analog triggers and a hat d-pad count too). A button already in use swaps places, so
every Wii U button keeps one. Also here: the rows for Jump anywhere, the moon jump, L1 and d-pad down
as item buttons. Home / Guide and holding Select always open the menu.

### Motion controls and rumble

Controls › **Motion controls** (gyro aiming) and **Rumble** work for touch and controllers. While a
connected controller and this device both have motion sensors (or rumble), the entry opens a page
with the source:

- **Gyro aiming from**: *Automatic* (the controller while you play with it, this device while you
  play by touch), *This device*, or *Controller*.
- **Rumble on**: *Automatic*, *This device*, *Controller*, or *Both* at once.

### Controller details

Controls › Controller › **Controller details**: what Android reports about each connected controller
(the one in use first): name, vendor and product IDs, connection, battery, rumble motors, motion
sensors, lights, buttons and axes. Handy when a controller doesn't behave as expected.

## The HUD: hearts, rupees, Picto Box

![The large Picto Box view](screenshots/picto-view.jpg)

Controls › **Screen (HUD)**:

- **Hearts only in combat**: the hearts fade out while the sword is put away and come back when it is
  drawn, when Link is hurt or heals. The magic bar shows by itself while magic is used.
- **Rupees only when they change**: the rupee count fades in when you collect or spend rupees.
- **Large Picto Box view** (Flexible with context buttons, on by default): the Picto Box's
  viewfinder fills the screen, pinch to zoom (the telescope too), the shutter and the other buttons
  sit at fixed places like a phone's camera app, and the album is browsed by swiping. Off: the game's
  original Picto Box and telescope screens.

## Picto Box album, postboxes and Link's clothes

| | |
|---|---|
| ![The large Picto Box view](screenshots/picto-view.jpg) | ![Album pages: a full album goes on to page 2](screenshots/album-pages.jpg) |

These are on the **Game** tab.

- **Album pages** (off): the Picto Box album gets any number of pages of 12 pictos. When the album is
  full, the next picto starts a new page. In the album, the bar at the top (or ZL / L on a
  controller) turns the pages. Your game save keeps the page in play as before (other programs can
  still read it); the other pages are kept in files next to the save.
- **Picto subjects** (on): in the album, a label above each picto names who it caught, with the

- **Mail pictos at postboxes** (on): at any Island Postal Service postbox, once you have the Picto

  figurine is put in the gallery the next day, no visit needed. If the Picto Box is on no button, it
  is lent to Y for the handover and Y's item comes back afterwards.
  - **Same day delivery** (off): the figurine is in the gallery at once.
- **Link's clothes** (As in the story): Hero's clothes or his casual clothes (the lobster shirt) at
  any time. The change happens in a puff of smoke once the menu is closed.
